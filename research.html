Research

Culturally adaptive AI durably reduces health misinformation

A randomized field study of how the character delivering health information changes what women believe, and for how long.

Results

A month-long field study with 434 women

Women were randomly assigned to watch Ganga Devi (188), watch Amy (162), or receive no intervention (84). We tracked their belief in myths such as "childlessness is a curse" and "pregnancy must be hidden for the first trimester" over one month.

434

women in a randomized, longitudinal field study in India

30%

less misinformation belief after watching Ganga Devi, vs. control

17%

less misinformation belief after watching Amy, vs. control

3 wks

later, the gains held and Ganga Devi kept her lead

A culturally adaptive character reduced misinformation belief nearly twice as much. The largest advantage was on community-reinforced beliefs, such as blaming women for infertility.
